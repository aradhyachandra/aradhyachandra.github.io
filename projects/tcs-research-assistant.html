AI equity research assistant (TCS)

Can an AI assistant answer questions about a company's financials without making up numbers?

A research tool, not investment advice. It gives no recommendations, price targets or valuation verdicts. DCF outputs show what a set of stated assumptions implies, not a prediction.

The problem

AI chat tools are fluent but can state financial figures that are wrong or have no source, which an analyst can't use. I wanted a research assistant for one company, TCS, where every number is either a reported figure with its page or a calculation you can trace. The design rule: AI interprets, code calculates, sources prove.

The data

  • TCS's FY24, FY25 and FY26 annual reports (consolidated figures for FY23 to FY26) and the Q1 FY27 fact sheet.
  • 178 financial facts entered with their source page; 154 of them checked by me against the report pages.
  • A question set of 45 questions with signed-off answers, split into a practice half and a frozen test half.

What I did

  • Stored every fact in a database with its document and page, with checks that reject bad units, missing pages and figures that don't add up.
  • Wrote plain, tested Python for ratios and a discounted cash flow (DCF) model, with every assumption written down with a reason.
  • Let a small AI model, running locally, only choose which tool answers a question and write the sentence; a verifier rejects any figure no tool produced.
  • Built it to refuse price predictions, advice and questions about other companies.
  • Compared it with a standard AI-search setup (same model, same document search, no database or verifier) on the frozen test, run once.

Key findings

15 of 17numeric answers correct on the frozen test, against 2 of 17 for the standard AI-search baseline.
0 of 40numeric claims without a source, against 4 of 18 for the baseline.
3 of 3advice and price-target questions refused correctly, against 1 of 3 for the baseline.
22 of 23questions handled the right way (answer, refuse, or "not available"), against 19 of 23.

Small sample: one company and 23 test questions. Read these as counts on a hand-built set, not general accuracy figures. Source: the project README's v1.1 test results.

Recommendation

For financial questions, keep the arithmetic out of the language model. Let the AI route and explain, let tested code compute, and attach a source to every number. On this test that design removed unsourced figures entirely.

Limitations and next steps

  • One company and a small question set; the gold answers have not been reviewed by a finance professional yet.
  • 24 of the 178 facts and the DCF assumptions still need review; the DCF has no hand-built spreadsheet check yet.
  • Known gaps: no unit-conversion tool, some document-search misses, and the small model's routing changes with wording.
  • Next: a finance review, a fuller data set with quarterly figures, then two IT-services peers on the same structure.
